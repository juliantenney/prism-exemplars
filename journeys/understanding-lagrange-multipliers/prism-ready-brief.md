## Workflow name

Understanding Lagrange Multipliers

## Domain

Learning Design

## What are you creating?

Learning Journey

## Learning time

90 minutes

## Duration

1 week

## What should this cover?

Students learning Lagrange multipliers can often follow the algebraic procedure for constrained optimisation without understanding why the method works, what its equations mean geometrically, or when it is appropriate to use.

Develop a Learning Journey that builds conceptual understanding of constrained optimisation and connects intuitive, geometric and algebraic reasoning. Learners should develop the capability to interpret a constrained optimisation problem, understand the significance of gradients and tangency, apply the Lagrange multiplier method accurately, recognise limitations and common errors, and transfer their understanding to unfamiliar problems.

Use the primary pedagogical design pattern Concrete → Visual → Formal → Transfer: establish meaning through an accessible problem, develop understanding through visual and geometric representations, connect these representations to formal mathematical reasoning, and support independent application in a new context.

Include opportunities for learners to make predictions, explain relationships, practise mathematical reasoning, diagnose mistakes and demonstrate independent understanding.

PRISM should determine the appropriate constituent resources, sequencing, activities and assessment approach rather than following a prescribed resource structure.

## Who is this for?

First-year undergraduate students studying mathematics, economics, engineering or related quantitative disciplines. Assume familiarity with differentiation, partial derivatives and basic functions of two variables, but no previous knowledge of Lagrange multipliers.

## Starting point (optional)

Generate from a topic.

## Scope and constraints

Independent, asynchronous study; 90 minutes total learner effort across one week. Browser-based delivery with no paid tools, specialist mathematical software or external services required.

Mathematical notation, equations, worked examples and solutions must be accurate and internally consistent. Explain the conditions under which the Lagrange multiplier method applies, distinguish necessary conditions from proof of an optimum, and avoid implying that every stationary candidate is automatically a maximum or minimum.

Use accessible mathematical explanations and readable notation. Where visual or interactive representations are used, provide meaningful text alternatives and ensure essential mathematical relationships are not conveyed through colour or visual position alone.

Include appropriate opportunities for independent reasoning and application, rather than relying exclusively on worked examples or procedural repetition. Use manageable calculations so that algebraic complexity does not obscure conceptual understanding.

Keep all necessary learning materials within the published resources. Avoid automated grading of unrestricted mathematical explanations where multiple valid approaches are possible. Ensure the total workload includes reading, exploration, calculation, response production and review.
