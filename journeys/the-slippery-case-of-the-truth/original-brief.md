# Original commissioning brief

The following is the original brief supplied to PRISM's Learning Journey Requirements step. Wording preserved verbatim.

## PURPOSE / FOCUS

Develop a Learning Journey titled “The Slippery Case of the Truth”, exploring philosophical theories of truth through an unfolding fictional detective investigation. Learners encounter conflicting witness accounts, documents and evidence, alongside philosophical perspectives that challenge how they interpret the case.

The central educational purpose is not to solve the mystery but to develop philosophical judgement: distinguishing truth from belief, evidence, justification, coherence, usefulness and interpretation. Learners should encounter correspondence, coherence and pragmatic approaches to truth, alongside relevant challenges concerning perspective and justification. Philosophers should function as intellectual interlocutors rather than simply deliver lectures; represent their positions accurately without suggesting that all theories are interchangeable or that all interpretations are equally true.

Use the detective narrative as a coherent vehicle for progressive disclosure, prediction, comparison, reconsideration and reasoned argument. Learners should make an initial judgement, confront challenges to it, compare philosophical explanations of the same evidence and produce a final reflective argument showing how their understanding has developed.

The principal design pattern is Narrative immersion → Philosophical disruption → Conceptual comparison → Reinterpretation → Reasoned judgement. PRISM should determine the appropriate resources, sequence, activities and assessment approach. The quality of philosophical reasoning matters more than identifying a culprit.

## LEARNERS / AUDIENCE

First-year undergraduate students from any discipline, including students with no previous formal study of philosophy. Assume curiosity and general academic reading skills, but no prior knowledge of theories of truth or philosophical argumentation.

## LEARNING TIME

90 minutes

## DURATION

1 week

## CONSTRAINTS / ADDITIONAL CONTEXT

90 minutes of independent, asynchronous learning across one week. An introductory undergraduate Learning Journey exploring philosophical theories of truth through a progressively unfolding fictional detective story. The scope should be sufficiently focused to allow meaningful engagement with three principal approaches to truth, their differences, limitations and implications for interpreting evidence, culminating in a reasoned philosophical judgement.

## DELIVERY AND ACCESSIBILITY

Independent, asynchronous browser-based study. All necessary narrative, philosophical explanations, evidence and activities must be contained within the published resources. No paid tools, external services, prior philosophical knowledge or specialist software. The experience must remain meaningful without audio, animation or visual-only clues. Use accessible text, readable layouts and meaningful alternatives for any essential images.

## NARRATIVE DESIGN

Use a single coherent fictional detective case that unfolds progressively. The case is a vehicle for philosophical enquiry, not a puzzle whose solution is the primary learning outcome. Introduce evidence, testimony, apparent contradictions and changing interpretations at carefully chosen points. Maintain internal consistency: distinguish newly discovered facts from changes in how existing facts are interpreted. Avoid revealing later evidence or resolving the case prematurely. The story should be engaging but economical enough to leave substantial time for philosophical reasoning.

## PHILOSOPHICAL INTEGRITY

Develop an accurate, accessible understanding of correspondence, coherence and pragmatic approaches to truth, with appropriate reference to philosophers associated with them. Philosophers may appear as fictionalised interlocutors in the detective narrative, but their ideas must be represented fairly and with sufficient nuance. Clearly distinguish truth from belief, evidence, justification, certainty and usefulness. Do not imply that all perspectives are equally valid, that truth is simply a matter of opinion, or that philosophical theories merely supply different guesses about the culprit. Avoid fabricated quotations or misleading historical claims. The central question is what it means for a claim to be true and how different theories illuminate or complicate that question.

## LEARNING AND ASSESSMENT

Create meaningful opportunities for learners to form an initial interpretation, explain why they believe it, encounter challenges, compare philosophical perspectives on the same evidence, revise their reasoning and construct a defensible final argument. Encourage learners to distinguish a claim's truth from the reasons available for believing it. Assess conceptual understanding, comparison, evaluation and justified philosophical judgement rather than success at solving the detective mystery. Allow reasonable disagreement where arguments are coherent and supported; do not automatically grade unrestricted philosophical explanations.

## PEDAGOGICAL DESIGN

The principal pattern is Narrative immersion → Philosophical disruption → Conceptual comparison → Reinterpretation → Reasoned judgement. Preserve an authentic opportunity to think before giving away a complete philosophical interpretation. Use progressive disclosure and purposeful reconsideration rather than repetitive comprehension questions. A Learning Journal may be useful for revisiting earlier learner-authored judgements, but it is optional and should be included only if it adds genuine developmental value. PRISM should determine the appropriate constituent resources, sequencing, activities and assessment design. Keep total reading, exploration, response production and review within 90 minutes.
