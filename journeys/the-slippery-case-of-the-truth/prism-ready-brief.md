# PRISM-ready brief — The Slippery Case of the Truth

Adapted mapping of the original commissioning brief onto PRISM’s current Workflow Basics form. The historical brief is preserved in `original-brief.md`.

## Workflow name

The Slippery Case of the Truth

## Domain

Learning Design

## What are you creating?

Learning Journey

## Learning time

90 minutes

## Duration

1 week

## What should this cover?

Develop an introductory Learning Journey exploring philosophical theories of truth through a progressively unfolding fictional detective investigation. Learners encounter conflicting testimony, documents and evidence, and use correspondence, coherence and pragmatic approaches to examine what makes a claim true. The aim is to develop philosophical judgement, not solve the mystery.

Use the progression: Narrative immersion → Philosophical disruption → Conceptual comparison → Reinterpretation → Reasoned judgement.

Learners should make an initial interpretation, confront challenges, compare theories, reconsider their reasoning and construct a defensible final philosophical argument.

PRISM should determine the constituent resources, activities, sequence and assessment.

## Who is this for?

First-year undergraduates from any discipline, including students with no previous formal study of philosophy. Assume general academic reading skills and curiosity, but no prior knowledge of theories of truth or philosophical argumentation.

## Starting point (optional)

Generate from a topic.

## Scope and constraints

Independent, asynchronous, browser-based learning: 90 minutes across one week. All narrative, explanations, evidence and activities must be self-contained; no paid tools, external services, specialist software or assumed philosophical knowledge. Use accessible text and layouts; no essential audio, animation or visual-only clues.

Maintain one internally consistent fictional detective case with carefully timed evidence disclosure. Distinguish newly discovered facts from reinterpretations of existing evidence. Avoid premature revelations. Keep the narrative economical and preserve sufficient time for philosophical reasoning.

Represent correspondence, coherence and pragmatic theories accurately, with appropriate philosophical references. Distinguish truth from belief, evidence, justification, certainty and usefulness. Avoid relativism, fabricated quotations and misleading historical claims. Philosophers should function as intellectual interlocutors rather than simply deliver lectures.

Include opportunities for initial judgement, conceptual comparison, revision and justified argument. Assess philosophical reasoning rather than identifying a culprit. Allow defensible disagreement; do not automatically grade unrestricted philosophical explanations.

Preserve independent thinking before supplying interpretations. Use a Learning Journal only where it adds genuine developmental value. Keep all reading, activities and review within 90 minutes.
