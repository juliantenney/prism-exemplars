# Adaptation consultation prompt — The Slippery Case of the Truth

Copy this prompt into an AI assistant when developing a Learning Journey brief for your own teaching context.

---

I want help drafting a commissioning brief for a PRISM Learning Journey.

**My subject / topic / teaching challenge:**
[Describe what you teach and the difficulty learners face. Start from your context—not from a named pedagogical pattern.]

**Who the learners are:**
[Level, discipline mix, what they already know]

**Time available:**
[Learning time and calendar span, if known]

Please help me clarify, in order:

1. **Intended capability** — what learners should become better able to do by the end.
2. **Where they typically struggle** — misconceptions, incomplete practice, feedback gaps, or transfer problems.
3. **What learners should produce or practise** — judgements, comparisons, revisions, reflections, or other evidence of learning.
4. **What feedback and improvement should look like** — including whether an early independent attempt should precede theoretical scaffolding.
5. **Constraints** — prior knowledge, tools, accessibility, whether work must be carried forward between activities, and what must not be automated or oversimplified.

Use the exemplar Learning Journey “The Slippery Case of the Truth” only as an illustration of one possible design logic:

- Teaching need: learners can name theories of truth without applying them when evidence conflicts or a case changes.
- Sequence idea: open with a contested case judgement → introduce witnesses/records and truth theories against that case → change the evidentiary picture → reconsider and defend a conclusion, with a journal preserving reasoning.
- Transferable idea: case-first judgement with delayed theory and forced reconsideration can make abstract concepts serve disciplined judgement; the subject matter and exact activities need not be copied.

Do **not** assume I want the same pattern, the same forensic narrative, or the same product mix. Propose a brief that fits **my** teaching challenge. If a case-first, reconsider-later structure seems suitable, say why; if another structure fits better, prefer that.

Return a structured commissioning brief I can paste into PRISM (purpose/focus, learners, learning time, duration, constraints), without inventing validation evidence or claiming the exemplar’s original brief as mine.
