# PRISM-ready brief — Explaining Research Findings with Confidence

Adapted mapping of the original commissioning brief onto PRISM’s current Workflow Basics form. The historical brief is preserved in `original-brief.md`.

## Workflow name

Explaining Research Findings with Confidence

## Domain

Learning Design

## What are you creating?

Learning Journey

## Learning time

90 minutes

## Duration

1 week

## What should this cover?

Develop the ability to interpret and communicate research findings accurately, clearly and appropriately for a non-specialist audience.

Learners should produce a short, evidence-based explanation of a research finding, evaluate the quality of contrasting explanations, and improve an initial attempt through comparison, feedback and revision.

Use a realistic, self-contained research example with enough numerical and contextual information for learners to distinguish findings from interpretations, recognise uncertainty, and avoid overstating conclusions.

Give learners opportunities to produce their own work, recognise differences in quality, make comparative judgements, revise their work and explain the reasoning behind their revisions. The educational focus is evaluative judgement and transferable communication skills, rather than arriving at a single correct answer.

PRISM should determine the appropriate constituent resources, sequence, activities and assessment approach.

## Who is this for?

Undergraduate students across disciplines, particularly those beginning to interpret and communicate research evidence.

## Starting point (optional)

Generate from a topic.

## Scope and constraints

Total learner effort: 90 minutes across one week. Assume no specialist statistical knowledge beyond basic percentages and comparisons.

Use a fictional but plausible research dataset or study, clearly identified as illustrative. Keep its figures, methods, limitations and conclusions consistent wherever reused. Supply all evidence needed to complete the activities; no external research or paid tools should be necessary.

Learners should make an initial independent attempt before encountering worked examples or detailed evaluative guidance. Later activities should support comparison, feedback, meaningful revision and reflection on changes made.

Learner-generated work may need to be retained and brought forward manually between resources; do not assume automatic cross-resource transfer.

Avoid automated grading of unrestricted writing or presenting one model response as the only acceptable solution. Support reasoned differences in interpretation while maintaining factual accuracy.

Delivery must be accessible through ordinary browser-based resources.
