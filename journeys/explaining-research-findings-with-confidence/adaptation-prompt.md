# Adaptation consultation prompt — Explaining Research Findings with Confidence

Copy this prompt into an AI assistant when developing a Learning Journey brief for your own teaching context.

---

I want help drafting a commissioning brief for a PRISM Learning Journey.

**My subject / topic / teaching challenge:**
[Describe what you teach and the difficulty learners face. Start from your context—not from a named pedagogical pattern.]

**Who the learners are:**
[Level, discipline mix, what they already know]

**Time available:**
[Learning time and calendar span, if known]

Please help me clarify, in order:

1. **Intended capability** — what learners should become better able to do by the end.
2. **Where they typically struggle** — misconceptions, incomplete practice, feedback gaps, or transfer problems.
3. **What learners should produce or practise** — drafts, comparisons, judgements, revisions, reflections, or other evidence of learning.
4. **What feedback and improvement should look like** — including whether an early independent attempt should precede worked examples or detailed guidance.
5. **Constraints** — prior knowledge, tools, accessibility, whether work must be carried forward between activities, and what must not be automated or oversimplified.

Use the exemplar Learning Journey “Explaining Research Findings with Confidence” only as an illustration of one possible design logic:

- Teaching need: undergraduates struggle to explain research evidence clearly for non-specialists and to improve that explanation through evaluative judgement.
- Sequence idea: interpret evidence → write a first explanation → compare contrasting examples against criteria → diagnose own work → revise → explain what changed, with a journal preserving learner-authored work.
- Transferable idea: draft → judge → revise can develop evaluative judgement; the subject matter and exact activities need not be copied.

Do **not** assume I want the same pattern, the same research-study stimulus, or the same product mix. Propose a brief that fits **my** teaching challenge. If a draft–judge–revise structure seems suitable, say why; if another structure fits better, prefer that.

Return a structured commissioning brief I can paste into PRISM (purpose/focus, learners, learning time, duration, constraints), without inventing validation evidence or claiming the exemplar’s original brief as mine.
