# Design notes — Explaining Research Findings with Confidence

These notes are **retrospective design commentary**. They are not the original commissioning brief. The original brief is preserved separately in `original-brief.md`.

## Extracted from the project

- **Title:** Explaining Research Findings with Confidence (from published package `<title>`).
- **Constituent products (6):** C01 Interactive — Interpret the research evidence; C02 Assessment Pack — Write your first explanation; C03 Interactive — Compare explanations and judge their quality; C04 Assessment Pack — Diagnose your explanation; C05 Assessment Pack — Revise your explanation; C06 Expository — Explain what changed and why. Plus a journey-level Learning Journal.
- **Learning sequence:** Overview → C01 → C02 → C03 → C04 → C05 → C06, with journal access for retained learner-authored work.
- **Learning time / duration (from original brief):** 90 minutes total learner effort across 1 week.
- **Published package:** unified Learning Journey shell (`published/index.html` + `runtime/` + `media/` including per-commission assets).

## Supplied by the user

- **Original commissioning brief:** PURPOSE / FOCUS, LEARNERS / AUDIENCE, LEARNING TIME, DURATION, and CONSTRAINTS / ADDITIONAL CONTEXT (verbatim in `original-brief.md`).
- **Primary pattern framing:** developing evaluative judgement through comparison, self-diagnosis and revision.
- **Supporting mechanisms:** initial independent performance; comparison of contrasting examples; explicit quality criteria; self-assessment and diagnosis; iterative improvement; reflection on changes; Learning Journal preserving learner-authored work.
- **Status:** `candidate` (no validation evidence asserted for this corpus entry).

## Retrospective commentary

- **Teaching need (lecturer-facing):** learners who can read a study still often cannot produce a clear, non-specialist explanation without overclaiming, and they lack structured practice at judging and improving such explanations.
- **Why this design addresses the brief:** the sequence separates interpretation, independent production, comparative judgement, self-diagnosis, revision and reflection—matching the brief’s requirement that learners attempt first, then compare, revise and explain changes, without automated grading of unrestricted writing as the sole success criterion.
- **Transferable principle:** reuse the draft–judge–revise evaluative-judgement logic with a domain-appropriate artefact; do not assume the research-study stimulus or these exact product types fit every subject.
- **Adaptation cautions:** keep a self-contained evidence base if the domain needs one; preserve the “independent attempt before heavy guidance” constraint; plan how learner work is carried forward between resources; avoid presenting one model response as the only acceptable solution.
