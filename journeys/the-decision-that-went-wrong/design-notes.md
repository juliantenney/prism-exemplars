## Retrospective commentary

- **Teaching need (lecturer-facing):** students need practice judging institutional options when early results look promising but later evidence and stakeholder impacts complicate the case—without treating the topic as an AI engineering course.
- **Mechanisms supported by the published design:** progressive disclosure; critical reading of quantitative/qualitative evidence; stakeholder perspectives; uncertainty and reconsideration; trade-offs; justified recommendation. These are recorded because they appear in the stage structure and activity titles, not because they were pre-declared as mandatory labels.
- **Transferable principle:** progressive evidence + justified choice under ambiguity travels to other high-stakes decisions; the fictional AI student-support scenario should not be copied as “a course about AI.”
- **Adaptation cautions:** keep one coherent case world; introduce evidence in order; preserve more than one defensible recommendation; avoid collapsing the task into a single “correct” policy answer.
