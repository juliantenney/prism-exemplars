# Original commissioning brief

The following is the original brief supplied to PRISM's Learning Journey Requirements step. Wording preserved verbatim as historical evidence.

For the current PRISM Workflow Basics form, use [`prism-ready-brief.md`](prism-ready-brief.md).

## PURPOSE / FOCUS

A fictional British university has introduced an AI-assisted student support system intended to identify students who may benefit from additional academic support. Initial evaluation findings appear promising, but further evidence raises questions about effectiveness, fairness, student experiences, reliability and unintended consequences. Learners will examine a developing institutional case, interpret conflicting quantitative and qualitative evidence, consider stakeholder perspectives and reach a justified recommendation under uncertainty. The scenario, documents, data and stakeholder accounts should be invented for this resource and remain internally coherent across the Learning Journey. The central theme is evidence-informed institutional decision-making, not technical AI development.

## LEARNERS / AUDIENCE

Undergraduate students across disciplines, with no assumed specialist knowledge of AI, data science, educational technology or university governance.

## LEARNING TIME

90 minutes

## DURATION

1 day

## CONSTRAINTS / ADDITIONAL CONTEXT

Independent, asynchronous study; 90 minutes total learner effort across the complete Learning Journey. Use a single coherent, explicitly fictional UK university scenario with consistent people, dates, evidence, figures and document identifiers across constituent resources. Include credible simulated institutional records, evaluation data and stakeholder accounts. Distinguish observation, association, causal interpretation, opinion and uncertainty. Introduce information progressively; avoid revealing later evidence or conclusions prematurely. Preserve genuine ambiguity and allow more than one defensible recommendation. Use accessible UK English, readable data representations and text alternatives for essential visuals. No specialist software, external services or prior technical knowledge required. The total workload must include reading, evidence examination, reasoning, response production and self-review; avoid unnecessarily extensive dossiers or repetitive tasks. Resources must work as standalone learner exports.
